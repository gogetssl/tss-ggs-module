1.7.0 (2026-11-14)
Migration from the legacy module
- Every legacy product moves to the new module; the legacy module is no longer
  kept for products the certificate provider has no equivalent for. Before the
  start, the migration page lists those products with their services and the
  administrator chooses for each:
  - an alternative product: existing certificates keep working and are renewed
    as the new product, or
  - keep as a record: the certificate and its order details stay visible to the
    client and the administrator, but it cannot be renewed, reissued or
    configured, it is never invoiced for a renewal, and the product is hidden
    and retired. Products without active or unconfigured certificates are kept
    as records without asking.
- A run either completes or is undone. Services that fail are tried once more;
  if any still fail - or the preparation or the switch fails - the run rolls
  itself back at once and the legacy module carries on as before. The 20% rule
  is gone: a switch can no longer leave a failed service between two modules.
- "Download error report" on a stopped run: versions, the services that failed
  with the reason, and the full log, with e-mail addresses masked - ready to
  send to support.
- A service whose own product code is unknown to the provider follows its
  product's mapping.
- A rollback also removes the settings the run added.

Other
- WHMCS lists TheSSLStore / GoGetSSL as the module's author; leftover vendor
  links and logos of the framework supplier are removed.

1.6.0 (2026-11-14)
Migration from the legacy module
- While a migration runs, the legacy module is paused for the services being
  moved: client actions and the SSL configuration pages (the service page
  can still be viewed and explains why), saving the service and its module
  commands in the admin area, the legacy crons, scripts and addon page, and
  its WHMCS hooks. A run that stopped with an error keeps it paused until it
  is rolled back. Services the run leaves on the legacy module are not
  affected.
- An order, renewal or upgrade paid during the run is held and runs
  automatically when the run ends - on the new module after the switch, on
  the legacy module after a rollback.
- The legacy module is paused before the backups are taken, so a legacy cron
  can no longer write half way through them.
- Large installs: the run indexes the tables it reads per service for its
  duration (10,000 services migrate in about 6 minutes, twice as fast as
  before). The module's own request table is now indexed by service.
- Tested with 10,000 services: run lock, a run killed half way and resumed,
  the stop when more than 20% of the services fail, orders placed during the
  run, and a full rollback.

Upgrade from 1.3.x or older
- The folder rename of 1.4.0 now also moves the module name stored on SSL
  orders and product addons, and in WHMCS's lists of active addons, module
  hooks and addon permissions. Orders placed before 1.4.0 could not be
  configured by the client while they kept the old name.

Other
- Encryption Everywhere is discontinued and no longer listed among the
  DigiCert brands.

1.5.2 (2026-11-14)
Renewal paid but still pending: a renewal the client paid whose payment has
not cleared yet (invoice "Payment Pending" - a bank transfer, an e-check).
Each product type now has a setting for what to do with it:
- SSL certificates (Finance page): wait 3, 7, 14 or 30 days after the order
  ends before the service is terminated as unpaid, or treat it as unpaid.
  Default: wait 14 days. Waiting costs nothing - the renewal is ordered only
  once the payment clears.
- Subscriptions (Automated SSL page): treat as unpaid (default - cancelled
  the day before the vendor renews and charges you) or wait 3 to 30 days.
  When waiting, the page warns that the vendor may charge you before the
  payment clears.
- The module no longer cancels a "Payment Pending" invoice when it
  terminates an unpaid service; it is left for you and noted in the
  activity log.

1.5.1 (2026-11-14)
A Sectigo renewal without "Additional Days For Renewal Order" set sends the
days left on the current certificate, and now never more than 30 - the most
Sectigo adds. A renewal paid on the first day of a 30-day lead counted 31.

1.5.0 (2026-11-14)
Renewals count from the end of the ORDER - the one to three years the client
paid for, or the day the vendor renews a subscription. The certificate's own
expiry is no longer used for it: inside an order the certificate is reissued
as often as needed.

Renewal invoices
- Each product type has two settings: create the renewal invoice N days
  before the order ends (at most 30 - a CA carries over no more than 30 days
  of the old certificate, so an earlier renewal would cost the client the
  difference) and a payment term of M days. Defaults: 30 and 14, so the
  invoice falls due 16 days before the end.
- The settings page says in words what the two mean together and warns,
  without forcing anything, when the due date lands too late: amber for a
  certificate due after its order ends, red for a subscription due on or
  after the day the vendor renews it (you would be charged first).
- With "Create Automatic Renewal Invoice" on, the module is the only one that
  invoices renewals: WHMCS's own invoicing is held back for these services,
  so a client never gets two invoices for one renewal. Off, WHMCS invoices
  them by its own Invoice Generation setting, as before.
- The invoice is created on or after its day: a day without a cron run no
  longer loses it.
- The due-date offsets of the Finance page are gone: a service falls due on
  its order end.

Placing a paid renewal - two independent settings
- "Renew Order Via Existing Order" decides only which WHMCS service carries
  the renewal: the same one (the expiring certificate stays downloadable
  until it expires), or a new one. A service renewed on a new order is billed
  no more - it used to be invoiced again the next year - and is completed once
  its own order has ended.
- "Renew With The Same Order Details" (one setting for every product; it
  replaces "Automatic Process of Renewal Orders" and the S/MIME and code
  signing switch): on, the renewal is sent to the vendor at once with the
  previous CSR, domains and contacts and the client gets "Certificate
  Autorenewed"; off, the client gets the new "Renewal Details Required"
  e-mail. When the vendor refuses the previous details, the client is asked
  for new ones instead of the renewal failing.
- Details submitted for a renewal are now ordered as the renewal of the
  previous order, so its remaining days are carried over. The client page
  says it is a renewal and until when the current certificate stays valid.
- A renewal the vendor will not link to the previous order - one paid long
  before the end, outside the vendor's renewal window - is ordered as a new
  certificate with the same details instead of failing.
- A renewal on a new service carries the paid period: the new service no
  longer starts with today's due date, which billed it a second time.

Migration from the legacy modules carries their renewal timing over as the
new pair, so every migrated client's due date stays where it was.

1.4.1 (2026-11-14)
Each edition calls itself by its own name everywhere. The line under the name
in Setup > Addon Modules said "module" for TheSSLStore Plugin, and the server
module could fall back to the other edition's name where nothing had loaded
the edition yet.

1.4.0 (2026-11-14)
The module is installed in a folder of its own name: TSSPlugin for TheSSLStore
Plugin, GGSModule for the GoGetSSL Module, in both modules/addons and
modules/servers. WHMCS knows a module by that folder, so the name now appears
wherever the module does - in the admin address of its pages, in the product's
server type and in the PHP namespace.

Upgrading from an earlier version, in this order:
- DELETE the old module folders first, both of them: modules/addons/ggsmodule
  and modules/servers/ggsmodule for the GoGetSSL Module, modules/addons/
  tssmodule and modules/servers/tssmodule for TheSSLStore Plugin. Nothing of
  yours lives in them; your data is in the database.
  For the GoGetSSL Module this order matters: PHP reads GGSModule and the old
  ggsmodule as the same name, so while both folders are there WHMCS stops with
  "Cannot redeclare ... Core\make()" on any page that loads the module;
- then upload the new modules/addons and modules/servers folders;
- open Setup > Addon Modules and activate the module under its new name. The
  products, their server type, the addon's settings and the API credentials
  are moved over for you; nothing has to be entered again, and no certificate
  or order is touched;
- if the module's cron runs from your crontab, change the path in it to the
  new folder.

Tables keep the names they were given in 1.3.0, and an install coming straight
from 1.2.0 or older has those renamed in the same step.

1.3.0 (2026-11-14)
Every database table of the module now carries the name of the edition it
belongs to: TSSPlugin_ for TheSSLStore Plugin, GGSModule_ for the GoGetSSL
Module. Until now they were named after the folder the module happened to be
installed in. An install made with an earlier version has its tables renamed
once, when the addon is activated or upgraded, or on its first cron run; no
data is copied or dropped.

The Commissions page is now Markups. Nothing was ever paid to anyone: the
feature adds a percentage to the price a client is charged, so it is called
what it is. The page, the menu entry, the stored rules and the texts follow,
and the per-product field keeps its meaning as the profit margin. Rules and
their percentages carry over untouched. A bookmark of the old page address
needs updating: mg-page=commissions became mg-page=markups.

1.2.0 (2026-11-13)
Every page a client sees now uses one design. The SSL order pages - waiting
for details, in validation, issued, cancelled - were still on the old layout
while code signing, S/MIME and the subscriptions had moved on; they now share
the same cards, badges and detail lists as the rest of the module.

The page a client lands on after paying was rebuilt to match: a header that
names the product and where the order stands, the four steps of an order with
the current one marked, the order and billing figures, and the choice between
cPanel autoinstall and manual enrolment as two cards instead of a pair of
buttons. The form keeps every field it had, with its sections numbered.

WHMCS's own product box and its empty Manage / Additional Information tab
strip no longer sit above the module's pages, so a client no longer sees the
product name, status and billing figures twice on the same screen.

1.1.1 (2026-11-13)
Security: the saved API Auth Token is no longer loaded into the settings form.
It was hidden on screen but still travelled in the page. The field now starts
empty and an empty field keeps the saved token.

Subscription domain slots now follow what the vendor actually charges for.
A slot is only paid for at the vendor when a domain goes into it, so:
- a renewal is charged for the domains in use, not for slots that were bought
  and never used, and those unused slots are not carried into the new period;
- an empty slot can be given back at any time, a slot holding a domain only
  while the vendor still refunds that domain (within 30 days of the day THAT
  domain was added, not of the day the slot was paid for);
- the domain list on the service page shows when each domain was added and,
  while it is still inside that window, until when removing it is refunded.
Buying more domains no longer sends a separate purchase to the vendor, which
that call never accepted for a subscription.

The admin dashboard counts every product type again: CodeGuard and document
signing orders were missing from the order pipeline.

1.1.0 (2026-11-13)
Document signing certificates of TheSSLStore (DigiCert Individual 500 / 2000 and
Organization 2000 / 5000). The client chooses on the service page whether the
certificate authority ships a hardware token or the certificate goes onto a token
they already have, fills in the organization and the administrator, and follows the
order from there; the service page shows the state, the vendor order and the token
set-up link. The certificate lives on the token, so no CSR is asked for and nothing
is downloaded. Includes the admin service tab, the order and issued e-mails, and
texts in all six languages.

1.0.0 (2026-11-13)
First release of the unified module, shipped as two packages from one code base:
GoGetSSL Module (GoGetSSL) and TheSSLStore Plugin (TheSSLStore). Each package
works with its own vendor account and can be installed beside the other.

Certificates
- SSL/TLS: order, reissue, renew, revoke; CSR generation and private key handling;
  DCV by e-mail, HTTP and DNS, with the approver address changeable afterwards.
- AutoInstall SSL and cPanel installation, including the queue that retries.
- ACME (Certificate-as-a-Service) and Plan + Automate subscriptions.
- S/MIME certificates, including TheSSLStore invite orders.
- Code signing on a hardware token or an own HSM, with the token and shipping sold
  as a product option; TheSSLStore Sectigo/Comodo and DigiCert products.
- CodeGuard website backup: the licence is created with the service, the client
  opens the CodeGuard dashboard from the service page, changes the contact, and the
  plan change, suspension and termination are carried out at the vendor.

Billing and subscriptions
- Renewal invoices, renewal reminders and automatic termination of unpaid services.
- Subscription products: renewal invoices for the whole period, prorated or full
  price when buying more domains, refunds inside the refund window, and credits for
  a period that is paid for but not started.

Administration
- Product import from the vendor catalogue, with prices, terms and SAN options.
- Dashboard with the order pipeline, certificate health and scheduled task state.
- Reports: sales, costs and profit per product, exportable as CSV.
- Module log of every gateway call, with secrets masked.
- Client area in English, German, French, Spanish, Czech and Danish.

Migration
- Takes over services from the legacy GoGetSSL (SSLCENTERWHMCS) and TheSSLStore
  (thesslstorefullv2) modules: products, settings, orders, subscriptions,
  AutoInstall state, S/MIME, code signing and CodeGuard, with a dry run first and a
  rollback if a run is stopped.
